Design and decision notes

The promise

Delphi is a voice companion in an older adult's home: no screen, no camera. This app is the one part that has a screen, for the people who love her. Family finds out if something is wrong. They do not get a transcript of the day it happened in. Every screen has to hold that: peace of mind that leaves her privacy whole.

Decisions

  • Alerts stays blunt, and the badge is rationed. Alerts is the one tab someone must find fast in an emergency, so speed wins over brand softness. The badge is a promise that this is worth interrupting you, so only urgent lights it. If amber items fired it too, people would learn it means little.
  • Family splits into two decisions. My notifications affect only me. Who has access affects Margaret and the whole family, so adding someone routes through her approval.
  • Call Margaret lives on Today. Inside an alert it implies you call when something is wrong. On Today it says checking in is just something you do.
  • One trust explainer, in Settings. "Urgent always reaches family, and Margaret is told at the same moment" should be findable in one place.
  • Introductions lives inside Today. A tab would present it as a signal that can go up or down in a way that matters. It is informational only. The copy never implies better or worse.
  • Memory Book has no add action. The stories are in Margaret's own words and she decides what is kept, by voice. Family cannot add or edit, so the card that looked like an add button is now a plain note.
  • Check-in and handled, kept simple. Both are reserved for urgent alerts on purpose. Only one check-in with Margaret is ever in flight, with a confirm step and a two hour wait after Margaret answers. Marking handled opens a short sheet: a quick pick, and an optional note, so the card reads "David called her" and the next sibling knows whether to act.
  • The handled sheet asks what actually happened. "How did you reach out?" assumes the person acted. When Delphi's check-in already settled it, they did not: Margaret answered, and nobody had to call. In that case the sheet asks "How was this resolved?", says when Margaret answered, and preselects "Delphi checked in", so it takes one tap and the log is true. The same happens when the care contact checked on her. The reach-out chips stay available for someone who also followed up themselves. With no check-in, nothing is preselected and the sheet is unchanged.
  • What Delphi did and what you can do are separate. The check-in result and Mark as handled used to blur into one block, because the result faded into the same off-white as the button. The result now has an outline in its own colour (green, lavender or red), a wider gap below it, and Mark as handled has its own edge. The family should never wonder whether tapping the tick is part of the result or a separate step.
  • Today shows Margaret's local time when the viewer is elsewhere. Many families live far away, so a call time only helps if it is in her clock, not yours.
  • Alerts are a permanent record: handled by, never seen by. The list shows who reached out and how, with their initial. It never shows who opened it. Read receipts between siblings turn a calm app into a scoreboard. Nothing can be deleted, because three people share the record and Margaret is told about urgent alerts anyway. Anything older than a week moves into History as a quiet log.
  • Delphi acting is lavender, and it is never silent. A check-in moves through Delphi is checking in, then either Margaret is fine or Couldn't reach her. The failure state always offers Try again and Call Margaret, and it never leaves the family with nothing to do.
  • Escalation is visible step by step. If a care contact is set and Margaret doesn't answer, Delphi calls them and the family sees calling, answered or didn't answer, then done. If they don't answer either, the next move is the family's: call them, call Margaret, or try again.
  • Two roles, one clear line of authority. Setting the care contact is the Organiser's call, seen by everyone so nobody is surprised by who Delphi tries next, but not editable by Members. Marking an alert handled has no approval step, since an emergency shouldn't wait on one; only the Organiser can add or remove people, and Margaret can remove anyone by telling Delphi. The Organiser role can be handed over: it asks first, says what the role allows, and the person handing it over becomes a Member, so there is always exactly one.
  • Settings is one gear. The Family tab holds the circle (people, roles, care contact). The gear opens My settings, which are personal: alert cadence, account, help.
  • Today shows how she is doing. Last spoke with Delphi, a good time to call, something to ask about from her own Memory Book, and who last called. Each one helps family connect. None of them says what she said.
  • The promise is said once, to first-time users. Two intro screens: what she says stays hers, and she’s in charge. Everyone else goes straight to Today. Pulling down at the top of Today refreshes the app and replays the opening.

What the research says

  • Most caregiver apps are care hubs: task lists, vitals, shared logs. In the caregiver-app reviews I read, the real problem is handoffs, where information gets lost between people. Delphi Family is small on purpose and answers one question well.
  • Both generations name privacy as their top concern, but they weigh monitoring differently. In a small US study of 18 mothers and 10 of their adult children (Berridge and Wetle, The Gerontologist, 2020), the children preferred every monitoring technology more than their mothers did and underestimated how much say the mothers should have. The sample was Meals on Wheels clients, so I read it as a direction, not a rate. It is why Margaret approves who has access, and why family gets a count of calls and never a word she said.
  • Alerts that cry wolf lose the home, so severity has to be rare to be believed.

Visual system (this pass)

  • 402 x 874 frame, SF Pro stack, 4 pt spacing, 20 pt margins, 44 pt tap targets.
  • Solid white content cards, one 20 pt radius, no gradients or coloured shadows.
  • One green accent (#6EB36D) for anything tappable, with a deeper green (#3A7A3A) for text and links so they remain readable. Red and amber appear only for alert severity. Handled and doing well use a deeper olive green and always carry a tick or a word, never colour alone.
  • Glass is used once, on the floating tab bar, plus the push banner as a temporary layer.
  • A status panel and the action below it never share a fill without an edge. The check-in result carries a 1 pt outline in its status colour, and the two rows above and below it carry a neutral one. Note the result panel still uses a soft tint, an exception to the no-gradients line above.

Still open

  • Settings covers alerts, display and accessibility, sign-in and app lock. Still not designed: managing signed-in phones (there is sign out, but no list of phones), leaving the circle, and what happens when an urgent alert goes unseen.
  • The two hour wait after Margaret answers a check-in, and the confirm step, are guesses. Two hours keeps a second check-in from catching her mid-nap and starting a false alarm, and it stops siblings tapping in turn. It needs real user testing. There is no wait after a missed check-in, because Try again is the point of that state.
  • Push suppression only checks the open alert screen. It cannot know about a backgrounded app or a second device.
  • Sunnyside Care Line is a placeholder, and every resolution (Margaret, the care contact) is a timer standing in for a real conversation or call, driven by the demo controls.
  • Who has access shows that someone has access, not what they are told. A son who wants falls but not mood dips cannot say so yet. Left alone until we know which categories families want split.
  • Handled notes are family-only: one pick (Delphi checked in, Care contact checked in, Called her, Spoke to a neighbour, Visited, or No action needed) and an optional one-line note. The first two appear only when that check-in happened. Open questions: should Margaret be able to see them? Should more than one pick be allowed, for someone who saw Margaret answer and also called her? And an answer with a concern in it is not modelled yet: the prototype only has answered and didn't answer, and it should not preselect "resolved" when Margaret answered but something was off.
  • The care contact chip says "Care contact checked in" and not the name, because the quick picks are a fixed list. Naming Sunnyside there would read better on the card.
  • The launch screen uses a placeholder mark, and the “Delphi Family” wordmark is plain app type. Delphi is a stand-in name, so there is no final identity to match.
  • Should family ever be able to suggest a Memory Book topic? It would work like the check-in (Delphi raises it, and tells Margaret who asked if she wonders), but it puts pressure on a space that is meant to be hers.
